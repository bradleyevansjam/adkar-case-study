Managing Change: The ADKAR Framework

A blended learning module that teaches people managers to diagnose where each team member is in a change, and how to help them move forward.

Audience
People managers leading change
Duration
90 minutes across 3 sessions
Format
Async pre-work, live session, follow-up
Approach
ADDIE, Bloom's taxonomy, Rise 360
Overview

Purpose

This module gives learners a working understanding of Prosci's ADKAR model and why it matters. It shows how change succeeds or stalls one person at a time, and why that should shape how managers plan, communicate and support change.

Who it's for

A mixed audience: employees going through change, and the managers and change leads guiding others through it. The content moves between the personal experience of change and the practical steps for leading it, so neither group sits through material meant only for the other.

How it's delivered

A hybrid design: online pre-work and follow-up around a facilitated in-person session. The digital parts are designed for Rise 360, with short readings, interactive knowledge checks and scenario-based reflection.

Module objective

By the end of this module, managers will be able to diagnose where team members are on the ADKAR change journey and apply targeted support strategies to help them progress.

Design rationale

Why it's built this way

Each design choice ties back to what managers need to do on the job: not just name the five stages, but spot them in real people and act.

1

Blended learning

ADKAR's Ability stage needs learners to practise making decisions. Async content alone can't build the skills managers need, so the core practice happens live.

2

Pre-work

Activating prior knowledge before the live session gives learners time to connect the model to their own experience. They arrive primed and knowing what to expect, not cold.

3

Branching scenario

Managers have to judge where an individual sits on the model, not just recall the stages. The scenario builds applied judgement at Bloom's Analyse level.

4

Job aid

A practical reference card carries the learning beyond the session and supports spaced application in the flow of work.

Content design

The five stages, through a manager's eyes

Each stage in the storyboard pairs a plain definition with what it means for managers and a facilitator prompt. Select a stage to explore it.

What it is

People understand that change is needed.

Remember · Understand

For managers, this means

  • Understanding the case for change
  • Recognising the impact on the team
  • Connecting the change to company strategy
  • Understanding their own role

Facilitator interaction

Live turn-and-share: “How is change communicated in your business today?”

Tug of War, agree or disagree: “Change is always necessary.” “All staff must be included in the process.” “Change is the responsibility of managers only.”

What it is

People commit to supporting the change. They must want it to happen, not just understand it.

Remember · Understand

For managers, this means

  • Model commitment yourself
  • Be honest about challenges but confident about success
  • Answer team questions openly
  • Acknowledge concerns without dismissing them

Facilitator prompt

“Think of a change you resisted, even though you understood why it was happening. What changed your mind?” Wait, and listen.

Desire is personal. A manager's job isn't to convince people the change is good; it's to be honest about what it costs and confident it's worth it.

What it is

The skills and information people need to carry out the change successfully.

Understand

For managers, this means

  • Assess your team's current skills
  • Identify who needs which training
  • Make sure everyone can access learning
  • Allow practice time without penalty

Key points to hit

Not everyone needs the same thing, so training should be matched to the person.

Knowledge without practice is just information. It doesn't stick.

What it is

People can actually perform the change. Knowledge without the ability to act creates frustration.

Apply · Analyse

For managers, this means

  • Be patient with early mistakes
  • Provide hands-on coaching

Facilitator notes

Don't expect perfection on day one. Celebrate when people get it right, even small wins.

What it is

Keeping people aligned with the change after rollout. Without it, people revert to old behaviours.

Evaluate · Synthesise

For managers, this means

  • Celebrate when people do it right
  • Keep reinforcing the “why” over time

Facilitator notes

Most training fails here: we celebrate completion, then disappear. Six weeks later, people are back to old ways.

Close with: “Ability and Reinforcement aren't the end of the change. They're when it becomes real, when it sticks.”

Session 1 · Pre-session

Awareness & Desire

Async20 minutesRemember · Understand · Analyse

Self-guided pre-work helps learners connect their own experience with the content and build a foundation in ADKAR before the in-person session.

Objective: managers will recall the five ADKAR stages and their sequence, understand how each stage shows up when team members resist change, and analyse a change in their own team to place a team member on the ADKAR journey. Measured by a quiz (at least 3 correct), a reflection naming a specific team change, and a self-assessed confidence check.

Scenario video

A manager watches a staff member resist a new process meant to improve performance. On-screen prompts follow:

  • What do you see happening here?
  • Why do you think it's happening?
  • How would you approach this situation?

Reflection board

Learners post reflections to an online discussion board, guided by questions under the video, to connect with peers and share their thinking.

Baseline survey

Captures how learners currently feel about guiding change, including comfort with hard conversations and sensitive topics, plus one change they're leading now and its obstacles.

Session 2 · Live training

Knowledge & Ability

In person60 minutesAnalyse

By the end of this session, managers will diagnose the ADKAR stage of at least two team members in a structured scenario, distinguishing between stages (especially Desire vs. Ability). Assessed through facilitator observation of the group activity, peer feedback and reflection.

Branching scenario · Try it

Meet Maya

The live session's centrepiece. Managers choose a response, then discuss what the outcome reveals about where Maya really is on the ADKAR journey.

Maya has worked in marketing at Justify Sciences for over 10 years. Her manager recently introduced a new marketing tool, Hootsuite.

Maya knows technology is changing and the marketing landscape with it. But she enjoys working with the system she knows and trusts. It's worked fine until now, so why change it?

What should you do?

Maya complies, for now.

Restating the policy treats this as an Awareness gap, but Maya already knows why the change is happening. The real barrier stays hidden, and she's likely to slip back to old habits.

This revealed where Maya really is.

Talking to Maya shows she's unsure how to start with the new system, worried newer colleagues will look more proactive, and overwhelmed by constant change. You reassure her that training, a coach and a mentor will be there. Maya isn't resistant; she's stuck at Desire. She wants to change but isn't sure how.

Trust takes a hit.

Escalating skips the conversation that would diagnose the problem. Maya feels reported rather than supported, and her Desire for the change drops further.

The manager reference card

A one-page job aid that turns the model into a diagnostic: spot the signs, name the stage, pick the action.

Manager Reference Card

ADKAR quick diagnosis
Stage
Signs your team member is stuck
What you can do
AAwareness
They seem confused about why the change is happening. They ask, “Why are we doing this?”
Share the business case clearly. Connect the change to outcomes they care about.
DDesire
They understand the change but resist it. They say, “I get it, I just disagree.”
Explore personal concerns. Listen before explaining. Address what matters to them.
KKnowledge
They want to change but don't know how. They ask process questions repeatedly.
Provide training, clear guidance and worked examples. Set up job shadowing.
AAbility
They know what to do but struggle in practice. They revert under pressure.
Create low-stakes practice. Coach in the moment. Reduce barriers to application.
RReinforcement
They've changed, but the behaviour is inconsistent. Old habits return over time.
Celebrate progress. Build in check-ins. Remove incentives for reverting.
Closing reflection

Before you go

A short reflection to consolidate learning. Themes feed into the team check-in the following week, linking the session to Reinforcement.

Q1

In your change initiative, which ADKAR stage is strongest right now, and which is weakest? Give a reason or evidence for your thinking.

Q2

Your team is resisting an upcoming system change. Using ADKAR, what should you do first?

Q3

What's one thing you'll do differently as a manager next week based on this learning?

Measuring impact

How success is evaluated

Before

Pre-work quiz (at least 3 correct), a reflection naming a real team change, and a baseline confidence survey.

During

Facilitator observation of the group scenario, plus peer feedback on each manager's stage diagnosis.

After

Reflection themes in the following week's team check-in, and spaced pulse checks to track whether new habits stick.
